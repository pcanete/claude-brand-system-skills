# Brand DNA Synthesis Rules

## Objective

Turn evidence into a reusable identity model.

## Avoid disconnected inventory

Weak synthesis:

Font: X
Color: Y
Photography: dark
Voice: premium

Strong synthesis:

The brand presents technical precision as cultural desirability.

This principle manifests through:

- concise authoritative language
- controlled editorial grids
- sculptural product photography
- restrained high-contrast color
- deliberate motion with little playful overshoot

## Core synthesis dimensions

Generate:

- strategic signature
- perceptual signature
- verbal signature
- visual signature
- photographic signature
- motion signature
- digital/interaction signature
- content signature
- product/physical signature when applicable

## Rule sets

Produce:

### Must preserve

Identity-defining.

### May adapt

Flexible.

### Must not introduce

Clearly incompatible.

### Channel-specific

Do not universalize.

### Campaign-specific

Temporary.

## Relationship graph

The most important synthesis step: connect strategic principles to actual
execution. Do not output a disconnected checklist.

Example:

PRINCIPLE:
Precision with cultural confidence.

VERBAL:
Short authoritative claims.

TYPOGRAPHY:
Tight, controlled display system.

LAYOUT:
Strict grid with deliberate exceptions.

PHOTOGRAPHY:
Technical objects treated as sculptural subjects.

MOTION:
Controlled and inertial rather than playful.

## Recognition model

Rank strongest distinctive assets.

Determine:

- minimum recognizable asset set
- strongest three assets
- strongest five assets
- generic category assets
- weakly owned assets
- potentially proprietary combinations

Recognition often does not depend on the logo alone. It may depend on:

specific type proportion
+ unusual crop style
+ one accent color

## Confidence

The final synthesis must clearly state coverage and weak-evidence areas.
